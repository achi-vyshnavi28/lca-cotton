Life Cycle Assessment — Virgin vs Recycled Cotton
What this is
A simplified cradle to gate life cycle assessment comparing virgin cotton against recycled cotton across CO2e, water use, and energy, built to answer one client style question: how much does switching from virgin to recycled cotton actually reduce footprint, and by how much depending on the blend.
Files
`Cotton_LCA_Virgin_vs_Recycled.xlsx` — the comparison model and blend calculator, 11 live formulas, 0 calculation errors on recalculation.
`memo.pdf` — the client ready recommendation memo summarizing the findings.
Methodology
Intended data source: Higg Index MSI (Sustainable Apparel Coalition / Cascale), the industry standard for exactly this comparison. Direct numeric access requires a Worldly/Cascale login that was not available for this project, so this model substitutes two independent, publicly documented, citable sources that corroborate the same Higg MSI finding, and says so plainly on the Cover and Methodology tab rather than presenting Higg MSI numbers that were never actually pulled.
Source 1: Recover™ verified, third party audited LCA of recycled cotton fiber.
Source 2: Liu et al, 2020, a peer reviewed academic study, used as an independent cross check on the same comparison.
Tabs in the model
Cover and Methodology — the data source substitution, explained, plus every assumption and its citation.
Comparison — virgin vs recycled cotton per tonne, across CO2e, water, and energy, from both sources side by side.
Blended Adoption and Calculator — footprint reduction at two realistic blend levels (65% and 80% recycled content), plus a calculator that takes an order quantity and recycled content percentage and returns the GHG and water saved.
Key findings
At a 65% recycled content blend: water use down 61.6%, energy down 46.9%.
At an 80% recycled content blend: water use down 77.9%, energy down 56.6%, GHG down 69.6%.
Worked example: a 10,000 kg order at 80% recycled content saves 13.84 tCO2e and 16,928 cubic meters of water versus the same order in 100% virgin cotton.
Recommendation in the memo: an 80% blend, not 100% recycled, balances footprint reduction against supply and quality constraints that a real sourcing decision has to account for.
Limitations
Figures are drawn from two corroborating public sources, not directly from the Higg MSI portal, because that required a login this project did not have. Treat this as directionally sound and well sourced, not as an official Higg MSI score.
The model is cradle to gate, meaning it does not cover use phase or end of life, which would require a fuller cradle to grave scope.
Resume line: Conducted comparative LCA of virgin vs. recycled textile inputs across CO2e, water, and energy using Higg MSI aligned data; presented as a client ready recommendation memo.
